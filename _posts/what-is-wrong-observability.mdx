In one of my previous roles, we were running Gluster FS on OpenShift. Every once in a while,
there used to be issues with Gluster FS.
Many of the issues could be resolved by following the runbooks and scripts we had. But once in a while,
the scripts and runbooks were not enough. In times like this, we typically involved the guy who setup Gluster FS.
It was a similar experience when it came to troubleshooting issues with F5 load balancers.
There were 2 or 3 people in our team who were experts in F5 load balancers and it went faster when they were involved.

Before you think this is a bad setup, I want to clarify that this is the fundamental idea behind open source also.

<PullQuote>Given enough eye balls, all bugs are shallow.</PullQuote>

Even large and successful open source projects like Rust and Kubernetes have only a few people who are responsible
for smaller components, curl which is one of the most used projects is maintained by one person and so on.

But I digress. Lets start with observability. I want to start with the definition, so we are thiking of the same thing.
I define observability as the ability to be informed ~~if~~ **when** thinks break as soon as possible,
have enough information to identify the root cause or a reasonable hypothesis of the root cause.
We want the information to be available for everyone in the team.
